I am a PhD candidate in Economics at Northeastern University. I am on the 2026–27 job market and will graduate in May 2027. My fields are health economics and labor economics.

I study how government policies affect economic opportunity and access to public services in historically marginalized communities. Governments try to help these communities through place-based policies or restructuring public services in an attempt to make them more efficient. My research examines whether these programs benefit the people they are intended to serve and how their costs are distributed.

I am always happy to connect and discuss new projects!

Job market paper

Healthcare Privatization and Patient Health: Moving to Medicaid Managed Care

Paper (PDF) Online appendix (PDF)

Governments increasingly rely on private firms to manage and deliver publicly financed health care, yet the consequences of large-scale privatization for patient health remain poorly understood. This paper examines Puerto Rico’s 1994–2000 health reform, which transformed a system resembling “Medicaid for all,” under which the government provided free health care to all residents, but through publicly operated facilities. The reform transferred government facilities to private providers and shifted eligible low-income residents into Medicaid managed care administered by private insurers and providers. Making a move from public to private management. Using a novel dataset of death records and newly digitized vital statistics, together with the staggered difference-in-differences estimator of Callaway and Sant’Anna (2021), I find that the reform increased deaths of residents under 65 by 11.2 percent relative to counties that had not yet implemented the reform. The reform also reduced ambulatory health care establishments and hospitals per 1,000 residents, suggesting that declining provider capacity may have contributed to the worsening health outcomes. These findings point to the potential health costs of large-scale transitions to privately delivered care and the importance of preserving provider capacity when restructuring publicly financed health programs.
